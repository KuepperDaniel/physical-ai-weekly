# Episode 1: Beyond the Robot Demo

<p>What turns a capable robot into a useful production system? This week on Physical AI Weekly, Sarah and Daniel examine advances in integration, contact sensing, computation, and safety—and the operating evidence still missing from the announcements.</p><h3>In this episode</h3><ul><li><strong>NVIDIA connects the stack</strong>: Updated Isaac tools link simulation, learning, and onboard execution, while more than 2,000 simulation-ready assets could reduce environment preparation. Shorter setup times remain a vendor claim, and the associated research humanoid platform is scheduled for early 2027—not deployed in a factory.</li><li><strong>Eka bets on force feedback</strong>: A $150 million Series A backs manipulation that combines vision with contact sensing. The company claims 1,500 handling operations per hour, but sustained customer performance, damage rates, and operating costs remain undisclosed.</li><li><strong>Schaeffler puts humanoids to work</strong>: Six humanoids across five stations assemble joint modules at its newly opened Taicang facility. Production monitoring and training could create a useful feedback loop, but output, uptime, intervention rates, and payback have not been published.</li><li><strong>AMD cuts simulated inference time</strong>: Published optimizations reduce model computation from roughly 223 milliseconds to nine on an AMD GPU, with similar reported simulation success. The roughly 25-fold gain is against unoptimized execution—not a measured improvement in physical robot cycle time.</li><li><strong>beRobox moves the safety zone</strong>: An arm-mounted Sonair ultrasonic sensor is intended to let palletizing continue at one station while an operator changes the other pallet. The demonstration does not establish measured throughput gains, and sensor certification does not replace complete-cell safety validation.</li></ul><p>The common test is sustained useful output after failed grasps, interventions, maintenance, and safety stops. Better building blocks matter, but their value depends on how reliably—and economically—the whole system works together.</p><h3>What we're watching</h3><ul><li><strong>Schaeffler’s production results</strong>: Component output and human intervention rates would help establish whether the Taicang approach is repeatable.</li><li><strong>AMD beyond simulation</strong>: Independent testing on physical industrial arms, against optimized alternatives, would clarify whether faster computation delivers practical operating gains.</li></ul>
