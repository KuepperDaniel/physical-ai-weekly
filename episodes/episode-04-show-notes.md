# Episode 4: Count the Results, Then the Robots

<p><em>Produced with AI: Sarah and Daniel are AI-generated voices, and the research and script are written by AI from public sources.</em></p><p>Warehouse results lead this week's look at industrial automation, followed by manufacturing investment, conditional humanoid deployments, Beijing's robotics programme and downloadable robot software. Each offers a different route to scale—and a different basis for business decisions.</p><h3>In this episode</h3><ul><li><strong>ASICS finds stock faster</strong>: Customer managers report roughly 60% faster misplaced-inventory recovery with Corvus One warehouse drones, alongside about 200 counting hours reassigned each month at its Mississippi distribution centre. Evaluate warehouse automation against inventory availability and recovery time as well as labour productivity, while treating these single-site figures as customer-reported results.</li><li><strong>Path funds expansion beyond welding</strong>: Path Robotics raised $300 million to extend its adaptive welding technology into additional manufacturing applications. For manufacturers handling variable parts, the funding signals growing supplier capacity in specialised automation, with local operating economics still central to any buying decision.</li><li><strong>GXO sets a conditional humanoid pathway</strong>: GXO and Apptronik signed a letter of intent contemplating up to 1,000 Apollo robots over four years, subject to performance, safety and operational milestones. Structure robot service agreements around measurable acceptance criteria so that deployment commitments follow demonstrated operating value.</li><li><strong>Beijing links capacity with qualification</strong>: Beijing's robotics programme targets annual manufacturing capacity of 200,000 robots and at least 20 industrial reference applications by the end of 2028, alongside shared testing infrastructure. Treat these targets as a potential shift in supplier availability and qualification requirements, with implementation determining their commercial significance.</li><li><strong>Runway opens robot software access</strong>: Runway made its robot model publicly downloadable for commercial adaptation on November 11, widening access beyond its partner programme. Use that flexibility to broaden engineering experiments and supplier choice, while requiring real-world validation before operational deployment.</li></ul><p>The strongest automation cases connect a specific task to a measurable business result. Funding, capacity targets and software access become valuable when they translate into dependable operations.</p><h3>What we're watching</h3><p>Whether GXO's letter of intent becomes binding service terms, turning a conditional fleet ambition into an operating commitment.</p>
